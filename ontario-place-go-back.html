Cadence spec — and a correction to rev 4

Stratify by generation and sample 30 days; the per-device machinery buys nothing here

Rev 4 built this as a per-device baseline over 300 days and reported two headline numbers that were artifacts of that window. With the design cadences you supplied — GEN1 5 day, GEN2 1 day — and a 30-day sample, both numbers are wrong and the conclusion reverses.

ClaimRev 4 saidCorrect figure, 30-day window
healthy p95 gap120 h25.7 h — matches the GEN2 1-day nominal at 1.07×
flat 48h ruleflags 71 of 74flags 0 of 74, and catches all 16 dark GEN2 meters

The 120 h figure came from a 300-day baseline that swept in older eras when these meters genuinely behaved worse. Sampling only the last 30 days — as you specified — the site sits right on its design cadence, and the case I made against a flat threshold collapses with it. Any cut between 30 h and 72 h separates this site perfectly: the worst healthy meter has been silent 28.1 h, the nearest dark one 76 h. Your existing rule of thumb — GEN2 over 48 h, GEN1 over 6 days — is well inside that band.

What does matter is the generation split. The two nominals differ 5×, so a single site-wide bound would flag every healthy GEN1 meter as dead. That is the one part of the machinery that is load-bearing.

The spec as it now stands

  1. Window. Last 30 days of radio contacts per device, per site. Contacts within 20 minutes collapse into one check-in. Liveness comes only from keys the radio must transmit — never meterValUpdated, which carries server-side stamps.
  2. Stratify. Split the site by generation. GEN2 reports VddAdc; GEN1 reports Vdda only. Nominal check-in 24 h and 120 h respectively.
  3. Throw out the outliers. Iterative robust trim on per-device p90 gaps, modified z-score cut at 3.5, so already-failing meters cannot inflate the bound they are about to be judged against.
  4. Bound. 2× the surviving cohort's median p90 gap — but only while that cohort still tracks nominal within 1.5×. If a whole site has drifted, the site term is distrusted and the bound falls back to 2× nominal, so a sick site cannot normalise its own sickness.
  5. Flag. SILENT if trailing silence exceeds the bound; ERRATIC if the device's own p90 gap exceeds it; SPARSE if there are fewer than 4 contacts to judge.
CohortBoundDerivation and result at this site
GEN2 ×9051.4 hcohort median p90 25.7 h, 11 outliers trimmed, drift 1.07× — site term trusted
GEN1 ×2240 hboth members dark, so no cohort — fell back to 2× nominal, as designed

So the honest value of the auto-calibrated version is not accuracy — a hand-set per-generation constant matches it here. It is that the bound is derived per site rather than hand-tuned, it reports when a site drifts off nominal, and it degrades safely when a cohort is too small or too sick to trust. Whether that is worth the complexity over your 48 h / 6 day rule is your call; the numbers say the simple rule is sufficient at Ontario Place.

Two dispositions still rest on it, and both hold: lots 62 and 137 are silent 99 h and 76 h against a 51.4 h bound, so they are genuinely flagged — but at only 1.9× and 1.5×, by far the mildest breaches on the property, which is why they stay on a morning re-check rather than the replacement list. Lot 89 is silent 318 h at 6.2× bound, so unlike rev 4's per-device version this spec does flag it outright.

Your rule, applied — with one adjustment

A raw FOTA-failure counter cannot be the trigger; terminal failure can

Treating failed-FOTA meters as external-antenna candidates is applied below. But taken literally the rule selects the whole property: 90 of 92 meters carry a non-zero fsm_fail_TIFOTA, including healthy ones — lot 6 sits at −107 dBm reporting 28 of 28 days with a count of 113. Update retries are normal background behaviour here.

So the trigger is terminal failure — the update never completed and the meter did not come back:

  • Download never completed — tifota_fetch_err of 3 to 63. Lots 11, 12, 89, 92, 102, 132, 147.
  • TI version latched at 0 on the update day and never returned. Lots 15, 95, 130, 134.
  • Retry loop that never exited — lot 100 at 71 attempts; lots 95 and 130 with tifota_hard_reset_cnt of 3740 and 1054.
  • BSL handshake failing — lot 43, with 84 init errors plus 46 password and 46 baud.
  • Died on the update itself with healthy cadence right up to it — lots 49 and 132.

That catches 14 of the 18 dark lots. Lots 31 and 153 do not qualify — dark 6 and 9 months on GenI-era firmware with no FOTA evidence — so they take standard units.

On the coverage hypothesis

Coverage does not explain the 16185 failures — the signal ranges overlap almost completely

You flagged that 16048 has a known issue and that FOTA failures can be coverage-driven. That mechanism is real, and lot 100 plus the high-fetch_err lots look exactly like it. But it does not explain who died here, and the 16185 rollout is a big enough sample to say so. Measuring each meter's RSRP in the 10 days before its own update:

A meter bricked at −113.5 dBm while another survived the same update at −127. If signal were the deciding factor those numbers would not cross. The 16048 sample is only 5 meters so it settles nothing either way — but lot 49 died on the 16048 update at −115 dBm with a 27-of-28 day cadence and a sound battery, which is hard to pin on coverage.

This does not change the work order — the antennas go on either way, and at these lots they can only help the next download complete. It changes what to expect from them: an antenna should not be assumed to prevent the next 16185 brick. Clearing the pins is what does that.

Order of operations

  1. Clear the seven 16185 pins before anyone leaves the shop. A replacement meter that attaches and pulls 16185 can fail the same way the old one did.
  2. At the 14 ext-antenna lots, swap the meter. At lots 11, 15, 43, 49, 92, 102, 132, 134 and 147 the battery was still sound, so a mag reset is a free shot first if you want to bring hardware home unused — but the update already failed at these doors, so do not count on it.
  3. Confirm every meter you touch reaches 17037+ before you leave the lot. In-sync-and-online is the only state that survives at this property.
  4. Lots 62 and 137: check the dashboard that morning. They breach the cadence bound, but at only 1.5× and 1.9× — the mildest on the property — and both are in sync with their pins. If they are back up, skip them.
  5. Do not touch the 10 no-action lots. They are metering and attaching on healthy batteries.
Reading the tables

Column notes

  • Last radio contact is the last time the meter actually transmitted — RSRP, RSSI, status or firmware version. It is deliberately not based on meterValUpdated, which carries server-side stamps: eight of these dark meters show a meterValUpdated write at 19:00 on 12–13 Aug, weeks after the radio went quiet. Using that key would have made half the dark list look alive.
  • RSRP final 30d is the median over the meter's last 30 days of contact, not a single last gasp. Fleet median for working meters is −112 dBm; for dark meters, −118.
  • VddAdc below roughly 3400 mV is abnormal — the 10th percentile across the 74 working meters here is 3605 mV.
  • Report days final 4wk is out of 28. A meter that held 24–28 right up to the day it died was healthy and then stopped; one that decayed to single digits was failing gradually.
